- # Slides Compatibility
	- `lsg` publishes Logseq's View as slides: the first item of the More menu adds a Slides card to the right sidebar with the `Tip: press f to go fullscreen` line and a 400px deck in reveal.js's structure, one column per top-level block with the block and then every descendant as its own slide, the same arrow controls, progress bar, keys, overview and fullscreen, and slide backgrounds and transitions from block properties. The deck is `lsg`'s own script, built from the page's published outline, which keeps only the current slide and its neighbours in the document. It leaves out blank slides, takes keyboard focus when it opens, lets `f` leave fullscreen as well as enter it, uses the garden's fonts, and has no menu item below 640px.
	  id:: da70cd63-2bda-445c-a145-b28b7e1760d7
	- Measured on 2026-10-26 against the `gh-pages` build of garden commit `083106d` (`gh-pages` `8132c95`, served locally, light theme, 1280×800 and 390×844, Playwright's Chromium) and an `lsg` build of the whole garden from `lsg` `48fcc54` (`mise run parity:build`, run `20261027T002614Z`). The OpenSpec change [match-logseq-slides](https://github.com/codekiln/logseq-gardener/tree/main/openspec/changes/archive/2026-10-27-match-logseq-slides) then built the deck, and [published-slides](https://github.com/codekiln/logseq-gardener/blob/main/openspec/specs/published-slides/spec.md) holds its requirements.
	- ## What Logseq's published site shows
		- Source links point at the local `logseq/og` checkout, `6e7afa8eb` (`frontend` = `src/main/frontend`). The live build ships reveal.js 4.6.0. Measured live values come first and win where they differ from the checkout or from [[lsg/Ref/Logseq Published Site/Clickable Prototype]]. The Prototype board's slide model, card and fullscreen overlay differ from the live build; the section Differences from the Prototype board below lists them.
		- ### The menu item
			- **Where.** `View as slides` is the first item of the header's More menu (`⋯`), and the same list appears when a reader right-clicks the page title ([`page_menu.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/components/page_menu.cljs#L118-L125), [`header.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/components/header.cljs#L85-L87), [`content.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/components/content.cljs#L328-L336)). The string is `:page/slide-view` in [`dicts/en.edn`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/resources/dicts/en.edn#L170-L171).
			- **When.** On any page route, including journal pages and pages that are not slide decks. It is absent on the home route and in the native mobile apps. The publishing guard that hides Favorite and Delete does not apply to it, so the published site shows it.
			- **Measured menu** on the AI Engineer Summit page: `View as slides`, `Export page`, a divider, `Export graph`, `Toggle theme`. On the home route: `Export graph`, `Toggle theme`. The menu is a `.dropdown-wrapper` 194px wide below `⋯`, background `rgb(241,243,245)`; items are 14px links, 36px tall, padding `8px 16px`, `rgb(104,112,118)`.
			- **Action.** The item calls `sidebar-add-block!` with the type `:page-slide-view`, so it behaves like every other right-sidebar card (the Shift-click section of [[lsg/Ref/Logseq Compatibility/Right Sidebar]]): the card goes to the top, the sidebar opens at 40% of the window (512px at 1280px), and the main page stays as it is.
			- **Phone width.** At 390×844 the item is still listed, but choosing it does nothing, because `sidebar-add-block!` does nothing below 640px.
		- ### The slides card
			- [`right_sidebar.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/components/right_sidebar.cljs#L173-L178) renders the card: the header is the page's original name as a link to the page (`a.page-title`), and the body is the deck from [`slide.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/extensions/slide.cljs#L63-L71).
			- A line `Tip: press f to go fullscreen` (14px, `f` in a `code` chip).
			- `div.reveal`, 400px tall, filling the card's width (440px in a 512px sidebar). reveal.js lays out a 960×700 slide and scales it to fit, `--slide-scale: 0.44` in that card.
			- The card has the usual header, `⋯` menu and close button. Its class is `item-type-page-slide-view`.
			- The card is saved with the other cards in `localStorage` `ls-right-sidebar-state`, as `["local" <database id> :page-slide-view]`. A reload restores the card and its deck, which loads reveal.js during start-up.
		- ### Loading
			- reveal.js is a separate file, `static/js/reveal.js` (92,420 bytes, 24.7 KB gzipped), loaded when the first deck mounts; a spinner shows meanwhile ([`slide.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/extensions/slide.cljs#L74-L87), [`gulpfile.js`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/gulpfile.js#L62)).
			- reveal.js's styles and its `black` theme are compiled into `style.css`, which every page loads ([`tailwind.all.css`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/tailwind.all.css#L13-L14)); the `.reveal` rules are about 59 KB of the 1.1 MB file.
			- Opening the AI Engineer Summit deck fetched `reveal.js`, the theme's Source Sans Pro regular and italic fonts (114 KB and 99 KB `woff`), both YouTube players the page embeds, and the page's images. reveal.js renders every slide at once, so media on later slides loads when the deck opens.
			- The slides come from the in-browser graph, at most 1,000 blocks of the page ([`slide.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/extensions/slide.cljs#L93-L95)). That graph is the 19.8 MB `db.gz` every Logseq page loads first; Logseq's fixture pages take 17 to 24 s to show their title on the desktop profile ([[lsg/Ref/Logseq Compatibility/Performance]]).
		- ### Slide structure
			- [`slide.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/extensions/slide.cljs#L47-L61) and [`slide.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/extensions/slide.cljs#L99-L107):
			- Each top-level block is a horizontal `section`.
			- A top-level block with children becomes a vertical stack: the block itself, without its children, is the first slide, and then every descendant at any depth is its own slide, in outline order. Depth is lost: a grandchild is the next slide after its parent, not part of it.
			- A top-level block without children is a single slide.
			- Text a block holds on its own lines, such as `*` list lines under a heading, stays on that block's slide. This is the structure the garden's `logseq-slides` skill writes: each nested block is one slide with `*` lines as its points.
			- The page's property block (`tags:: [[Logseq/Slideshow]]`) is the first top-level block, and because slides hide properties, it is a blank first slide. An empty top-level block is also a blank slide.
			- Journal pages drop the first item of the tree ([`slide.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/extensions/slide.cljs#L96-L98)); on `2026-10-23 Fri` both top-level blocks, `Filed` and `Updated`, still appear as columns, so the dropped item is not a content block.
			- Measured on `AI/ES/25/ws/What I Learned at the AI Engineer Summit Workshops 2025`: 7 columns with 1, 17, 6, 34, 7, 1 and 9 slides, 75 slides in all. Column 1 is the blank property slide and column 6 a blank block. Column 2 starts `What I learned at the [[AI Engineer Summit 2025 NYC Workshops]]`, then `What is the [[AI Engineer Summit]]?`, then `How I found out about the AI Engineer Summit...` alone on its slide, then the `flash back` block with its image.
			- A later measurement on the same `gh-pages` build found that the deck ends inside the workshop 3 block, at `Feedback Loops from Online Evaluators`, just before that block's second embed of the same block, so the rest of workshop 3, the workshop 4 block and the last image never appear.
		- ### Slide content
			- Each slide renders its block with the normal block component and `:slide? true` ([`block.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/components/block.cljs#L2842-L2932)):
			- No bullet, collapse arrow, drag handle, selection, task checkbox or marker ([`block.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/components/block.cljs#L2003-L2004), [`block.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/components/block.cljs#L2914-L2915)).
			- No property rows ([`block.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/components/block.cljs#L2362-L2368)).
			- Everything else as on the page: headings keep their tag (`h1` to `h4`) and the small level label (`h3`) Logseq shows beside them, page references keep the garden's brackets, images, block references, embeds and YouTube players render.
			- Nothing limits a slide's height. On the card, the `flash back` slide's image sits below the 400px deck and is cut off; fullscreen shows it.
		- ### Properties become reveal.js attributes
			- [`with-properties`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/extensions/slide.cljs#L18-L28) writes each block property as an attribute on the slide's `section`: `key::` becomes `data-key`, and `data-key::` stays `data-key`. reveal.js reads the ones it knows.
			- Measured on `Logseq/Doc/slide`: `background-image:: https://images.unsplash.com/…` becomes `data-background-image` and draws the image behind that slide. Headings add `data-heading="1"` or `"2"`, numbered lists `data-logseq.order-list-type="number"`, and the property block `data-tags="Logseq/Slideshow"`; reveal.js ignores those.
			- The garden's documentation of this is [`Logseq/Doc/slide`](https://github.com/codekiln/logseq-encode-garden/blob/main/pages/Logseq___Doc___slide.md), copied from Logseq's [`slide`](https://github.com/logseq/docs/blob/master/pages/slide.md) page.
		- ### Navigation and keys
			- reveal.js is started with `embedded`, `controls`, `center`, `transition: "slide"`, `history: false` and `keyboardCondition: "focused"` ([`slide.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/extensions/slide.cljs#L32-L43)).
			- **Focus.** Keys reach the deck only after a click inside it, which adds the class `focused`. Before that, arrow keys do nothing to the deck. Logseq's own up and down shortcuts stand aside while the deck is focused ([`editor.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/handler/editor.cljs#L3243-L3251)).
			- **Controls.** Four arrow buttons at the bottom right, `aria-label` `previous slide`, `next slide`, `above slide`, `below slide`, faint when disabled. A progress bar runs along the bottom.
			- **Keys** (reveal.js 4.6.0 defaults): Left or `h`, Right or `l` move between columns and return to the top of the column; Up or `k`, Down or `j` move within a column; `n` or Space next slide, `p` or Shift+Space previous; Shift with an arrow jumps to the first or last slide; `b` or `.` pauses with a black screen; `g` jumps to a slide by number; Escape or `o` toggles the overview; `f` enters fullscreen.
			- **URL.** Moving through slides leaves the URL unchanged.
		- ### Fullscreen
			- `f` asks the browser to put the `.reveal` element in fullscreen; the slide scales to the screen (`--slide-scale` 1.097 at 1280×800).
			- Pressing `f` again does not leave fullscreen. In the measured Chromium, Escape toggled the overview and fullscreen stayed on; a desktop browser normally leaves fullscreen on Escape at the browser level, which was not measured.
		- ### Look, light theme
			- Deck background `rgb(255,255,255)`. Body text `rgb(104,112,118)`, from `--ls-primary-text-color` ([`slide.css`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/extensions/slide.css#L1-L39)); headings `rgb(0,0,0)` through the light-theme override, and upper case from reveal.js's `black` theme. On the 960×700 slide an `h3` is 50.4px.
			- The garden's [`logseq/custom.css`](https://github.com/codekiln/logseq-encode-garden/blob/main/logseq/custom.css) adds `.reveal` rules for code blocks: 0.55em text, no height limit, light and dark colours.
			- Dark-theme values were not measured.
		- ### Differences from the Prototype board
			- **Structure.** The board makes each direct child of a top-level block one slide that includes all of that child's descendants. The live build gives every descendant its own slide.
			- **Card and fullscreen.** The board draws a 4:3 deck with its own controls, a `Fullscreen (f)` button and a fixed overlay that Escape or `f` closes, with the label `Slide {h.v} · arrows to move · f or Esc to exit`. The live build uses reveal.js's controls in a 400px-tall card and the browser's fullscreen mode.
			- **Keys.** The board's deck takes keys when it has keyboard focus (`tabIndex=0`). The live deck has no `tabindex` and needs a click.
			- The board's menu item is followed by a divider; the live build puts `Export page` after it and the divider after that.
	- ## What lsg publishes
		- On a page or journal page, from 640px wide, `View as slides` is the first item of the More menu; it is absent on the home route and the All pages route. Choosing it adds a Slides card for the page to the top of the right sidebar, expanded, and opens the sidebar; a Slides card for the same page already present moves to the top. The main page, its scroll position and its URL stay the same. [[lsg/Ref/Site Navigation]] describes it for readers.
		- The card's header title is the page's title as a link to the page, with Logseq's `presentation` icon, and its card menu offers the page-card items, Open as page included. Its body shows `Tip: press f to go fullscreen`, with `f` in a `code` element, above a deck 400px tall that fills the card's width. A Slides card is saved and restored with the other cards, and a restored one loads `lsg-slides.js` after the page's `load` event.
		- The deck has one column per top-level block. A column holds the block itself without its children as its first slide, then one slide for every descendant at any depth, in outline order. A block whose slide would show nothing once bullets, property rows and children are removed makes no slide, and a column with no slides is dropped. The AI Engineer Summit deck has 8 columns holding 17, 6, 34, 7, 1, 22, 18 and 1 slides, 106 in all, and the fourth slide of the first column is the `flash back` block with its image. `2026-10-23 Fri` gives 2 columns, starting `Filed` and `Updated`.
		- The deck is built from the page's published outline: from the live document for the current page, and from the published page file, through the sidebar's route cache, for another page. It fetches no other garden data.
		- Each slide shows its block's content as the page shows it, without bullet, collapse control, property rows or child blocks: headings at their level, text lines the block holds on its own lines, images, page links, block references, embeds and code blocks. Links inside a slide behave as links inside any card, so Shift-click adds a card. Slides have no height limit, so a tall slide is cut off in the 400px card and shown whole in fullscreen.
		- The deck lays out a 960×700 slide scaled to fit and centred, with the structure `div.reveal > div.slides > section`, so the garden's `custom.css` `.reveal` rules apply: a code block in a slide gets the 0.55em font size. In the light theme the deck background is white, body text uses the page text colour (`rgb(104,112,118)`), and headings are black and upper case at the sizes Logseq's build shows; an `h3` is 50.4px.
		- For each block property whose key starts with `background-` or `data-`, or is `transition`, `transition-speed`, `auto-animate` or `visibility`, the block's published item carries `data-slide-<key>`, with a leading `data-` in the key written once. The properties stay hidden from property rows. The deck copies each to the slide's `section` as `data-<key>`; `background-image`, `background-color`, `background-size` and `background-position` set the slide's background, and `transition` the slide's transition. On `Logseq/Doc/slide`, the `Logseq's slide feature is powered by` slide and the `To add a background image` slide show their background images. Pages without such properties gain no bytes.
		- The deck has four arrow controls named `previous slide`, `next slide`, `above slide` and `below slide`, faint when they lead nowhere, and a progress bar whose width is the current slide's position over the last slide's position. Pressing Down three times from the start shows the `flash back` slide, the fourth of 106, with the progress bar at 3/105 of the deck's width.
		- The deck takes keyboard focus when its card opens and after a click inside it, and receives keys only while focused. Left or `h` and Right or `l` move to the top of the previous or next column; Up or `k` and Down or `j` move within a column; `n` or Space and `p` or Shift+Space step through the whole deck; Shift with an arrow goes to the first or last slide in that direction; `b` or `.` blacks out the deck until the next key; `g` opens a field that takes a slide number or `column/slide` and goes there on Enter; Escape or `o` toggles an overview of every slide where a click or Enter chooses one. The shell's and sidebar's shortcuts are ignored while the deck has focus, and the URL never changes.
		- While the deck has focus, `f` puts the deck's `div.reveal` in the browser's fullscreen mode and scales the slide to the screen, and `f` in fullscreen leaves it. Escape leaves fullscreen through the browser.
		- `lsg-slides.js` holds the deck's behaviour and styles and is never requested before a Slides card renders. Only the current slide and its neighbours in each direction are in the document outside the overview, so images and players on other slides load only when the reader moves near them.
		- The SDK supplies each block's properties with their shown or hidden flag. The publisher writes the `data-slide-*` attributes in `site/outline.rs`, the menu item in `site/shell.rs`, the Slides card type in `lsg-sidebar.js`, and the column order, deck, controls, keys and fullscreen in `site/slides.js`.
	- ## Differences from Logseq
		- The deck is `lsg`'s own script, keeping reveal.js's structure and class names (`div.reveal > div.slides > section > section`, `.controls`, `.progress`, `.present`, `.focused`). reveal.js is 24.7 KB gzip and renders every slide and its media at once; the own script renders only nearby slides, which keeps memory bounded, and matches every reveal.js behaviour Logseq configures. reveal.js features Logseq does not set up, such as speaker notes, plugins, PDF export, fragments and the URL hash, are absent in both.
		- Blocks that show nothing make no slide. Logseq shows a blank slide for the page's property block and for empty blocks, which carry no content and which a reader has to step past.
		- The deck holds every block of the published outline. Logseq's AI Engineer Summit deck stops before the second embed of one block in the workshop 3 block, so it has 75 slides where `lsg` has 106.
		- The deck takes keyboard focus when its card opens, so a keyboard reader can present without a mouse. Logseq needs a click before keys reach the deck.
		- `f` toggles fullscreen. In Logseq's build `f` only enters fullscreen.
		- The deck uses the garden's font stack, which names Source Sans 3 and Source Sans Pro first. Logseq loads reveal.js's 213 KB of Source Sans Pro font files.
		- Only properties that change a slide reach the markup, as `data-slide-<key>`. Logseq writes every block property as an attribute on the slide.
		- Below 640px the menu item is absent, since the right sidebar does not exist there. Logseq lists the item and does nothing when it is chosen.
		- The page title has no right-click menu, so View as slides is only in More.
		- Logseq's dark-theme values were not measured; the deck uses `lsg`'s theme tokens in the dark theme.
	- ## Performance
		- Logseq builds any page's deck from the whole graph, which every page loads first, and adds reveal.js and two fonts. `lsg` builds the deck from the page's own outline, so opening slides fetches no garden data, and a page's first paint gains only the menu item.
		- The `[slides]` table of [`scripts/parity/budgets.toml`](https://github.com/codekiln/logseq-gardener/blob/main/scripts/parity/budgets.toml) holds the budgets: `lsg-slides.js` with its styles at most 6 KB gzip, choosing View as slides to the first slide at p95 within 300 ms on the desktop profile, one step to the next slide drawn at p95 within 50 ms, and JS heap growth of at most 10 MB after opening the AI Engineer Summit deck and stepping through every slide. Every fixture page's first paint stays within the `[desktop]` and `[phone]` budgets, and the `[shell]` `script_kb` and `[sidebar]` `script_gzip_kb` budgets cover the menu item's handler and the Slides card type. `parity:budgets` checks the sizes in CI. Phones have no Slides card, so there are no phone rows.
		- [[lsg/Ref/Logseq Compatibility/Performance]] records the run of 2026-10-27 on the AI Engineer Summit deck on desktop: 106 slides, 83 ms to open at p95 of 5 fresh windows, 13.9 ms per step at p95, 1.5 MB of heap growth, 2 slides in the document, and `lsg-slides.js` 4.5 KB transferred.
	- ## Before the change
		- The `lsg` build measured on 2026-10-26, from `48fcc54`, had no View as slides. These facts come from `crates/logseq-gardener-sdk/src/site/shell.rs`, `outline.rs`, `properties.rs` and the [published-app-shell](https://github.com/codekiln/logseq-gardener/blob/main/openspec/specs/published-app-shell/spec.md) specification, checked against the whole-garden build. The [app shell design](https://github.com/codekiln/logseq-gardener/blob/main/openspec/changes/archive/2026-10-25-match-logseq-app-shell/design.md) reserved the top of More for this item.
		- **No menu item.** `#lsg-more-menu` held `Toggle theme` and `Use system theme`. The specification said the menu contains only those items and that the header holds only controls whose destination exists.
		- **No right sidebar.** Its analysis, [[lsg/Ref/Logseq Compatibility/Right Sidebar]], was written, and nothing was built yet.
		- **The deck's content was already on the page.** The outline was nested `ul.outline > li` items, one per block, with headings as `h1` to `h4` inside the item, images with `loading="lazy"`, and each block's source position in a `source-block-N` span.
		- **Properties.** Block properties rendered as visible rows unless `properties.rs` marked the key as internal (`heading`, `background-color`, `id` and others). No property reached the markup as an attribute a slide could read. On `Logseq/Doc/slide` the `background-image::` value appeared nowhere in the `lsg` page.
		- **Media.** The AI Engineer Summit page had 23 images and no video player.
		- **Sizes.** The AI Engineer Summit page was 70,276 bytes (19.5 KB gzipped), of which 23,623 bytes were the inlined head and shell before `<main>`. `lsg-shell.js` was 5,662 bytes of the 6 KB `[shell]` budget. No page script mentioned slides or reveal.js.
