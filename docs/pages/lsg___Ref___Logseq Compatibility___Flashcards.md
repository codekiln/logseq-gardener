- # Flashcards Compatibility
	- `lsg` publishes Logseq's flashcards: card blocks with Logseq's shadow and a faded `card` tag, the left sidebar's Flashcards item with its due badge, `{{cards}}` deck widgets with the deck header, counter and first card, cloze, and Logseq's review dialog with its phases, preview and random modes, deck menu and SM-5 scoring that lasts for the visit, and it never shows the six `card-*` scheduling properties. Page counts are fixed when the site is built and corrected from the reader's clock when a review opens, cards with the same schedule come in page-title then outline order, a `[[Card]]` reference is hidden whole in the review, and other block properties such as `favorite-deck::` stay hidden, where Logseq shows them.
	  id:: de5a50b9-db94-4f3f-8196-b236419e9c11
	- Measured on 2026-10-27 against the `gh-pages` build of garden commit `083106d` (`gh-pages` `8132c95`, served locally, light theme, 1280×800, Playwright Chromium, browser clock 2026-10-27T04:33Z) and an `lsg` build of the whole garden from `lsg` `48fcc54` (`mise run parity:build`, run `20261027T002614Z`). On the encode garden there are 432 cards, 430 of them due on 2026-10-27, and 29 `{{cards}}` macros on 27 pages. The OpenSpec change [match-logseq-flashcards](https://github.com/codekiln/logseq-gardener/tree/main/openspec/changes/archive/2026-10-28-match-logseq-flashcards) then built the flashcards, and [published-flashcards](https://github.com/codekiln/logseq-gardener/blob/main/openspec/specs/published-flashcards/spec.md) and [garden-card-decks](https://github.com/codekiln/logseq-gardener/blob/main/openspec/specs/garden-card-decks/spec.md) hold their requirements.
	- ## What Logseq's published site shows
		- Logseq treats every block that references the `card` page (`#card` or `[[card]]`) as a flashcard. Its published site offers three views of them: a `Flashcards` item in the left sidebar with a count of cards due, which opens a review modal over every card; a `{{cards QUERY}}` macro, which shows a small deck header and the first card's front on the page, or `No matched cards`; and the card blocks themselves, drawn with a shadow and a faded `#card` tag. The scheduling properties that Logseq writes under each card (`card-last-interval::`, `card-next-schedule::` and four others) are never shown.
		- Source links point at the local `logseq/og` checkout, `6e7afa8eb` (`frontend` = `src/main/frontend`). Measured live values come first and win where they differ from the checkout or from the reference boards. The boards disagree with the live build in several places:
			- [[lsg/Ref/Logseq Published Site/Flashcards Review]] draws a 760px white modal with both `1/430` and `[overdue]/430` counters, a `Hide answers` button beside the scoring buttons, and a visible `#card` tag on the card front. The live modal is 836px wide and grey, shows one counter, has no `Hide answers` button while scoring, and hides the tag.
			- [[lsg/Ref/Logseq Published Site/Clickable Prototype]] draws the `{{cards}}` widget as a full inline reviewer, lists cards in page-source order, gives the modal no Close button, and says scoring writes nothing. The live widget shows only the first card's front and a `Review cards` button. Cards are ordered by next review date, the modal has a Close button, and scoring changes the counts until the page reloads.
			- Both boards use red, blue and green scoring buttons (`#ce2c31`, `#3e63dd`, `#18794e`). The live colours are listed under Review modal below.
		- ### What counts as a card
			- A card is a block whose references include the page `card`, written as `#card`, `[[card]]` or `[[Card]]`, since page names ignore case ([`extensions/srs.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/extensions/srs.cljs#L35-L47), [`srs.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/extensions/srs.cljs#L128-L132)). The card's front is the block. Its back is the block's children.
			- A card can contain `{{cloze …}}`, which adds a third phase, `Show clozes` ([`srs.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/extensions/srs.cljs#L222-L248)). The encode garden has no cloze.
			- Measured: `All` reads `430/432`, so the garden has 432 cards. A static count of blocks referencing `card` outside code also finds 432 lines in 268 pages and no journals. 264 cards carry scheduling properties.
		- ### Due cards and the counters
			- A card is due when it has no `card-repeats` or fewer than 1, no `card-next-schedule`, or a schedule before the reader's current time ([`srs.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/extensions/srs.cljs#L291-L308)). The due cards are sorted by the `card-next-schedule` text, so cards that have no schedule come first.
			- The deck header shows `due/total` (tooltip `Overdue/Total`), with `due` counting down as the reader scores cards. In preview mode it shows `index/total` (tooltip `Current/Total`) ([`srs.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/extensions/srs.cljs#L643-L659)).
			- Measured counts on 2026-10-27: `All` `430/432`, `[[VSCode]]` `71/73`, `(and [[VSCode/Keyshort/Git]] )` `9/10`, `[[yazi]]` `18/18`. They change as the reader's date passes each `card-next-schedule`.

		- ### What scoring does in the published build
			- Scoring computes the next interval with SM-5 and writes the six `card-*` properties back into the block ([`srs.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/extensions/srs.cljs#L315-L358)). `Forgotten` also schedules the card for tomorrow. When a `{{cards}}` deck is finished, Logseq inserts a `Summary: N items, N review counts [[date]]` block after the macro ([`srs.cljs`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/src/main/frontend/extensions/srs.cljs#L366-L383)). 			- The argument is a query in Logseq's simple query language, joined with `AND` to "references `card`". A page reference matches on the block's path references: the block's own references, those of its parent blocks, and its page ([`deps/db/…/rules.cljc`](https://github.com/logseq/og/blob/6e7afa8eb/deps/db/src/logseq/db/rules.cljc#L140-L143)). The page does not show the answers. 		- ### Performance in Logseq
			- The Flashcards count, the review modal and every `{{cards}}` widget need the whole graph in the browser. The build downloads `static/js/db.gz` (19.8 MB) and about 8 MB of script, then runs a database query per deck. On the desktop run, with no throttling, the Flashcards item appeared 23.0 s after navigation and its count 0.7 s later. [[lsg/Ref/Logseq Compatibility/Performance]] records 17.4–23.8 s to the title on desktop and 185–205 s on the phone profile across its fixture pages, with a peak JS heap near 2,000 MB, before any flashcard view can render.
	- ## What lsg publishes
		- ### Cards and decks
			- The SDK's `cards` module returns as cards the blocks of selected visible pages whose references include `card`, in any case and written `#card`, `#[[card]]`, `[[card]]` or `[[Card]]`, outside code blocks and inline code. Each card's children are its back. On the encode garden at commit `083106d` it returns 432 cards.
			- The SDK lists the argument of every `{{cards}}` macro as a deck query, an empty argument meaning `All` and a bare name a page reference, and selects a deck's cards by evaluating `(and <deck query> [[card]])` with the same query evaluator that renders `{{query}}`, over path references. The encode garden has 29 deck queries; `[[yazi]]` holds 18 cards, including cards under a parent block tagged `[[yazi]]`, and `(and [[tmux]] (not [[oh-my-tmux]]))` holds 71.
			- A card is due by Logseq's rule. Due cards are ordered by the `card-next-schedule` text, unscheduled first, with ties in page-title then outline order. For 2026-10-27T04:33Z, `All` is `430/432`, `[[VSCode]]` `71/73`, `(and [[VSCode/Keyshort/Git]] )` `9/10` and `[[yazi]]` `18/18`, and the first due card of `All` is on `1Password/Quick Access`.
			- The six `card-*` keys are hidden built-in properties: no page, reference, embed, search record or review shows them, and the graph view makes no pages of them.
		- ### Card blocks
			- A card block carries Logseq's `shadow-md` box shadow, padding `12px 0`, margin-bottom 8px and radius 4px. A `#card` tag or a `[[card]]` or `[[Card]]` reference is drawn at opacity 0.3. `card` counts as one of Logseq's built-in pages, so the tag stays unlinked and the build reports no unresolved-tag diagnostic for it.
		- ### Left sidebar item
			- The left sidebar lists Flashcards between Journals and Graph view unless `logseq/config.edn` sets `:feature/enable-flashcards? false`, with the `infinity` icon, the `g f` hint and a rounded badge with the number of cards due at build time, left out when none is due. A click, `g f` or `t c` opens the review of all cards. [[lsg/Ref/Logseq Compatibility/App Shell]] describes the rest of the sidebar.
		- ### `{{cards}}` widget
			- `{{cards QUERY}}` with matching cards renders `.cards-review` with the `.cards-title` header (the infinity icon, the query text, a caret, the `due/total` counter titled `Overdue/Total`, and the `Toggle preview mode` and `Toggle random mode` buttons) in Logseq's measured styles, then the first due card's breadcrumb and front without its children and with the card tag hidden, then `Review cards`. The header, counts and first card are static HTML computed at build time.
			- Activating the widget opens the review for its deck; the preview and random buttons open it in that mode.
			- A query with no matching cards renders a block at opacity 0.6 with `Cards: <query>` as inline code, then `No matched cards`, as `Logseq/Flashcard/Review/Favorite/Zoxide` shows.
		- ### Review dialog
			- The dialog has Logseq's measured size, colours, radius and Close link: an 836px panel at desktop widths, full width on phones, and a 740px card area at least 60vh tall. Its header repeats the deck header, and its caret opens `Switch to`, which lists every garden deck query and then `All`, 30 entries on the encode garden.
			- Each card shows its breadcrumb and front with `#card`, `[[card]]` and `[[Card]]` hidden. The phases are `Show answers` (`s`), `Show clozes` when the card has clozes, then `Forgotten` (`f`), `Took a while to recall` (`t`) and `Remembered` (`r`) in Logseq's measured colours with key chips.
			- Preview mode shows every card with `Show answers`/`Hide answers`, `Next` (`n`), `Reset` and a `Current/Total` counter. Random mode shuffles the remaining cards. After the last card the dialog shows Logseq's finished message. Close, Escape, `g f` and `t c` dismiss it.
			- On open, the dialog recomputes due cards from the reader's clock and updates its counter, the deck header and the sidebar badge, so a site built on 2026-10-20 and opened at 2026-10-27T04:33Z shows `430/432` for `All`.
		- ### Scoring
			- Scoring runs Logseq's SM-5 rules with the default settings and keeps the new schedule in memory for the visit. The counter, the deck header and the badge fall by one, and `Forgotten` schedules the card for the next day. Closing and reopening the dialog keeps the visit's scores, a reload restores the published counts, and nothing is written to browser storage.
		- ### Cloze
			- `{{cloze answer}}` renders `span.cloze` showing `[...]`, and `{{cloze answer \\ cue}}` shows `(cue)`. On a page, activating a cloze reveals `[answer]`; in the review, `Show clozes` reveals every cloze on the card. A card in `tests/fixtures/parity-garden/` covers cloze, since the encode garden has none.
		- ### Loading
			- The build writes `flashcards/decks/<id>.json` for each deck and for `All`, holding card numbers in due order and each card's schedule, and `flashcards/cards/<n>.html` with rendered cards in shared batches of 20, each card written once.
			- `flashcards.js` and `flashcards.css` load only when a reader activates a deck widget or the Flashcards item, or presses `g f` or `t c`, through a loader in `lsg-shell.js`. Opening a review fetches the deck file and the batch holding its first card, then the next batch before the reader reaches it. A reader who opens a deck page and does not interact with it loads no deck file, card batch or script.
		- The publisher renders the sidebar item, the card styles, the widget, the dialog, the deck and card files, cloze and the scoring script from the SDK's answers, so the CLI and the language server can use the same deck operations.
	- ## Performance
		- The `[flashcards]` table of [`scripts/parity/budgets.toml`](https://github.com/codekiln/logseq-gardener/blob/main/scripts/parity/budgets.toml) sets the budgets: `flashcards.js` at most 8 KB uncompressed and `flashcards.css` at most 1.5 KB gzip; the `All` deck file at most 8 KB gzip and each card batch at most 30 KB gzip; the fixture garden's deck page `Tools/Flashcards` growing by at most 4 KB gzip and staying within the `[desktop]` and `[phone]` first-paint budgets.
		- Clicking `Flashcards` shows the first card of `All` within 300 ms on desktop and 1,500 ms on the phone profile, against the 23 s before Logseq's item appears; the phone JS heap after reviewing 50 cards stays within 20 MB; and a whole-garden build is at most 10% slower than the `--baseline-lsg` binary.
		- `parity:budgets` checks the sizes in CI, and every fixture page keeps its size when the fixture garden gains 2,000 pages. `parity:perf --flashcards --record` measures the review on a copy of the whole garden and writes its section of [[lsg/Ref/Logseq Compatibility/Performance]]; that page holds no flashcards section yet.
	- ## Differences from Logseq
		- Page counts and each widget's first card are fixed at build time, using `SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH` when it is set, and corrected from the reader's clock when a review opens. Logseq computes them in the browser after loading the whole graph; shipping every schedule to every page would put card data on first paint.
		- Cards with the same `card-next-schedule` text, including the 168 unscheduled cards, come in page-title then outline order. Logseq keeps the order its database returns, which follows when each block was created in the desktop app and cannot be read from the files, so its first `All` card is `Lazygit/Keyshort/Commit` and `lsg`'s is on `1Password/Quick Access`. Every count matches.
		- A `[[Card]]` reference is hidden whole in the review. Logseq hides only the link and leaves `[[]]`.
		- Finishing a deck inserts no `Summary:` block after the macro. Logseq's published site inserts one into its in-memory copy, which a reload discards.
		- An empty `{{cards}}` is `All`, which `Switch to` already lists last, so it adds no entry.
		- `lsg` shows no block properties, so `favorite-deck::` and `deck-exclude::` stay hidden, where Logseq shows them under the macro's block.
		- The `card` page itself is not published, so the `card` tag is unlinked.
		- Logseq's editing actions have no counterpart: `Make a Flashcard`, the `/cards` and `/cloze` commands, and the SM-5 settings `:srs/learning-fraction` and `:srs/initial-interval`.
		- Logseq's dark-theme values were not measured; the styles use `lsg`'s theme tokens.
	- ## Before the change
		- The `lsg` build measured on 2026-10-27, from `48fcc54`, rendered flashcards from `crates/logseq-gardener-sdk/src/site/render.rs`, `site/shell.rs`, `garden_settings.rs` and `references.rs`.
		- **Sidebar.** The left sidebar held Journals, All pages and the favorites (`site/shell.rs`). No Flashcards item, no badge, no `g f` or `t c`. `garden_settings.rs` parsed `:feature/enable-flashcards?`, but nothing read it.
		- **`{{cards}}`.** `macro_fallback` in `site/render.rs` sent `cards` and `cloze` to the literal fallback. The yazi deck page showed `{{cards [[yazi]]}}` as inline code, and the build recorded an "Unsupported macro (including embeds and queries); preserved as literal text" diagnostic.
		- **Card blocks.** `#card` rendered as an unlinked `span.tag`, because no `card` page was published. The build reported 564 "Unresolved or excluded tag: card" diagnostics. `[[Card]]` rendered as `span.page-reference.unresolved`. Card blocks got no class or style of their own. Tags used `lsg`'s own `.tag` style (0.9em, opacity 0.8), not Logseq's 0.3 for `card`.
		- **Scheduling properties.** Hidden, but only because the outline renderer skipped every `Block::Properties` (`render.rs`, the `match block` arm near line 532). `favorite-deck::` and `deck-exclude::` were hidden too, though Logseq shows them. The page-level list in `site/properties.rs` (`internal`) did not name the `card-*` keys.
		- **What the SDK had.** `ReferenceIndex` in `references.rs` gave `linked(page)` (the blocks that reference a page, with namespace ancestors added) and `path_refs(block)` (the block's pages, its ancestors' pages and its page). The SDK had no evaluator for `and`, `or` and `not`, which the garden's decks use.
		- **Size.** The yazi deck page was 26,585 bytes. The source of all card blocks and their children, without `card-*` lines, was 194,868 bytes (66,381 gzip) of Markdown.
